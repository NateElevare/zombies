# Current art direction

The active legacy top-down game uses **authored, gritty pixel characters and effects against textured, desaturated city materials**. Current player sheets in `art/source/player-pixel/` are the human style reference; zombies and bosses have matching authored sheets. Campaign NPCs must use `art/source/campaign-pixel/`, not the old procedural humanoid generator.

- Legacy camera: directly overhead, facing east (+X), centered character pivot, 128px human frames at 0.5 world scale. Use nearest-neighbor sampling for pixel actors and campaign props.
- Look: charcoal contours, readable cloth folds and hair, worn materials, muted teal/olive/ochre/rust/mauve. Upper-left lighting; warm amber light belongs to powered Haven.
- Unique story objects use authored overhead assets. Shared beds, storage and furniture reuse the city's existing atlas artwork. Preserve proportions; do not stretch a short wall into an entire dam.
- `npm run art:campaign` imports saved PNGs and never calls the procedural rig. See `art/source/campaign-pixel/README.md` and `notes/art-consistency-audit.md`.
- Arcade's approved art and gameplay remain the baseline. The angled camera is a separate pipeline; do not place legacy sprites into it.

The sections below document the **historical procedural/3D pipeline**. Their asset names, geometry and attachment contracts remain useful, but the old style requirements do not override the active direction above.

# Historical art recipes and asset contracts

All art is generated by TypeScript in `art/`: `npm run art` runs `art/build.ts`, which calls every generator (characters are 3D models rendered by Three.js in a headless browser, §A2). Nothing is downloaded, traced or bought. The look we want is a **grounded, gritty, semi-realistic top-down survival game**. Think late-night horror: desaturated colors, careful soft shading, believable materials and readable silhouettes. It must not look like flat vector shapes or pixel art.

## Global rules
- **View:** straight top-down (90°). The camera is overhead, so we see heads, shoulders and the tops of objects. Key light comes from the **top-left**, with a soft shadow down and to the right, baked into props and characters at about 35% opacity.
- **Facing:** every character and weapon sprite faces **right** (+x = angle 0). The renderer rotates them.
- **Scale:** `ART_SCALE = 2`. Textures are drawn at 2× world size and displayed at 0.5 (for crisp HiDPI and zoom). A player with a 14 world-px radius is a ~56px-wide body inside a 128×128 frame.
- **Canvas:** `@napi-rs/canvas` (Canvas2D API in node) for everything except the characters, which are pre-rendered 3D (see §A2). Use layered drawing: base shape, shading (radial/linear gradients), material noise, edge highlights, a dark outline (soft, 1.5–3px at 2×, never pure black: use dark desaturated hues) and the baked drop shadow.
- **Determinism:** use a seeded RNG (implement mulberry32 in `art/lib/`) so `npm run art` is reproducible.
- **Palette:** desaturated. Skin ranges from pallid to sickly grey-green on zombies, with dried dark-red blood (#5a0f0f to #8a1a14). Clothes are muted denim, olive, brown, grey, faded red and navy. Asphalt is #2b2d2f to #3a3c3e; grass is dull, dry green #3d4a2c; concrete is #6b6a66.
- **Atlases:** Phaser JSON-hash format, `{ frames: { name: { frame:{x,y,w,h}, rotated:false, trimmed:false, spriteSourceSize:{x:0,y:0,w,h}, sourceSize:{w,h} } }, meta: { image, size:{w,h}, scale:"1" } }`. Max 4096×4096 per atlas page; split into more atlases if needed. Pad 2px between frames and extrude edges for tiles, so filtering doesn't bleed.
- **Shared library:** `art/lib/` holds the canvas helpers, RNG, noise, packer, atlas writer, manifest builder and shading helpers. The characters agent and the environment agent coordinate through it: one agent creates it first and the other reuses it. Each agent's generators live in separate files.

## Output contract (what the renderer relies on)
- `public/assets/<atlas>.png` and `public/assets/<atlas>.json` hold the atlases.
- `public/assets/manifest.json` is merged from per-generator fragments by `art/build.ts`:
```jsonc
{
  "artScale": 2,
  "atlases": ["characters", "zombies", "fx", "env_tiles", "env_props", ...],
  "sprites": {                              // per-frame-prefix metadata (optional fields)
    "player_torso_handgun": { "atlas": "characters", "origin": [0.5, 0.5] },
    "weapon_pistol": { "atlas": "characters", "origin": [0.2, 0.5], "muzzle": [44, 0], "grip": [0, 0] }   // offsets in WORLD px from the player center, facing right
  },
  "tiles": { "asphalt": ["tile_asphalt_0", "tile_asphalt_1", ...], "grass": [...], ... },  // 128×128 frames (64 world px), seamless
  "props": { "furniture:couch": { "frames": ["prop_couch_0", "prop_couch_1"], "fit": "stretch|contain|tile9" }, "car:sedan": {...}, ... }
}
```
- `public/assets/anims.json` is `[{ "key": "zombie_walker_0_walk", "atlas": "zombies", "frames": ["zombie_walker_0_walk_0", ...], "frameRate": 10, "repeat": -1 }, ...]`.
- `public/assets/icons/<itemId>.png` are 96×96 inventory icons (shown at 48), one for **every** item id in `legacy/js/sv/sv-items.js`, plus `icons/struct_<type>.png` for each structure in `legacy/js/sv/sv-base.js`.

## A. Characters, zombies and FX (CHARACTERS agent)
**Player** (atlas `characters`, 128×128 frames). It is built in two layers, so torso and legs can rotate independently:
- `player_legs_walk_0..7` is the feet/legs stride cycle, seen from above (boots alternating forward/back). `player_legs_idle_0` is the standing pose.
- `player_torso_<pose>_<frame>`: head (hair/beanie visible from the top), shoulders, arms, jacket. Poses:
  - `handgun_idle_0`: two-hand grip, arms forward.
  - `handgun_shoot_0..1`: recoil.
  - `handgun_reload_0..5`.
  - `long_idle_0`, `long_shoot_0..1`, `long_reload_0..5`: rifle/SMG/shotgun grip, one arm forward on the handguard and one at the stock.
  - `melee_idle_0` and `melee_swing_0..5`: arms sweep from upper-left to lower-right.
  - `unarmed_idle_0` and `unarmed_punch_0..3`.
  - `walk_bob`: none. The renderer adds a subtle sway.
- Armor overlays are drawn on top of the torso, using the same rotation:
  - `armor_head_<id>` for cap, bike_helmet, riot_helmet, military_helmet.
  - `armor_body_<id>` for hoodie, leather_jacket, police_vest, military_vest.
  - `backpack_<id>` for small, hiking, military.
- `weapon_<id>`: each weapon (pistol, revolver, smg, shotgun, assault_rifle, hunting_rifle, knife, bat, machete) as a held top-down sprite, with `grip`/`muzzle` metadata in the manifest (world px from player center, facing right, in the idle pose).

**Zombies** (atlas `zombies`, plus `zombies2` if needed). Types and footprint radius in world px: walker 15, runner 13, brute 24, crawler 13, bloater 20. Use frame size 128 (brute and bloater 192).
- Each type has **variants**: walker 4, runner 3, brute 2, crawler 2, bloater 2. Vary clothing color/type, hair or bald, skin tone, wounds and blood patterns.
- Per variant `v`, frames are `zombie_<type>_<v>_<anim>_<i>`:
  - `walk`: 8 frames. This is a real shambling gait seen from above: arms reaching forward and swaying, shoulders rolling, legs/feet stepping out from under the body. Runners lean forward with a faster stride. Crawlers drag their arms with no legs. The bloater waddles and its belly pulses.
  - `attack`: 6 frames. Arms lunge and claw forward, the head jerks.
  - `hit`: 2 frames. A flinch back.
  - `death`: 8 frames. It collapses forward/back and the limbs splay.
  - `corpse`: 1 frame, the final lying pose with a blood pool. It stays on the ground as a decal.
- Each walk frame must differ visibly: the body bob, alternating arm and leg positions. Animations must read clearly at 50% display scale.
- The bloater adds `zombie_bloater_<v>_pulse_0..3` (glowing pustules).

### A2. Pre-rendered 3D characters (default since v0.3)
The player and zombies are now **real 3D models rendered offline to sprites** (`art/characters3d/index.ts` + `art/chars3d/`). Everything is still generated by our own code: no downloaded models, textures or mocap. The 2D canvas generator (`art/characters/`) is kept for comparison and rollback: `ART_CHARS=2d npm run art` (or `npm run art:chars2d`).
- **Models** (`art/chars3d/web/rig.ts`): Three.js primitives (ellipsoids, tapered limb segments, tori, lathes, extrusions, rounded boxes) assembled into an articulated humanoid in meters (X forward, Y left, Z up). A `Look` sets proportions (`BUILD`: hero, human, lean, brute, bloat, crawler), clothing, hair and gore; a `Pose` sets pelvis/chest/head rotations plus **two-bone IK** targets for hands and feet. Weapons (`weapons.ts`) and armor/backpacks (`gear.ts`) are modelled the same way.
- **Animation** is procedural (`player.ts`, `zombies.ts`): walk cycles from phase functions (stride, foot lift, heel/toe pitch, hip yaw/roll, shoulder counter-rotation), attack/hit/death curves, and the bloater swell/glow.
- **Materials** (`materials.ts`): `MeshStandardMaterial` patched with object-space procedural detail (cloth folds and wrinkles, denim wash, leather creases, knit ribs, skin mottling and veins, wood grain, brushed metal), blood/grime/torn-cloth masks, a soft rim term and **per-vertex analytic sphere ambient occlusion** (every body part is approximated by spheres; plus a height falloff, since seen from above lower surfaces sit in the body's shadow). Detail stays at >= 3 cm so it does not alias at sprite resolution.
- **Camera/light** (`main.ts`): straight down from 12 m with a narrow FOV (heads/shoulders ~14% larger than feet, so arms and shoulders show a little of their sides and read as volumes). `PPM0 = 121` texture px per meter at the ground (= ~60 world px per meter; ~15% above true scale so the figures keep the on-screen presence of the old 1.3x 2D art). Constants live in `art/chars3d/web/consts.ts`. Overhead-dominant lighting: hemisphere sky light, a steep key from the top-left with soft self-shadows (PCF), a cool fill; ACES tone mapping. Because the key is near-vertical, rotating sprites in-engine never looks obviously wrong. Weapons render with an orthographic camera at hand height (1.3 m).
- **Post** (in the page): 2x supersampling, a soft contact shadow (blurred silhouette, ~34%) and a thin dark outline for readability at 0.5 display scale.
- **Rendering** (`driver.ts`): the web code is bundled with esbuild and run in headless Edge/Chrome over the DevTools protocol, by default on **SwiftShader** (software Vulkan via ANGLE: identical output on every machine, no GPU/driver dependence, deterministic). `ART3D_ANGLE=d3d11` uses the GPU instead (slow first shader compile on D3D). `ART3D_WORKERS` (default 3) sets parallel browser instances. ~800 frames render in ~100 s.
- **Atlases**: frames are **trimmed** (`art/lib/trimpack.ts`, Phaser JSON-hash `trimmed: true` + `spriteSourceSize`), so frame boxes can be generous (192–352 px) while the atlases store only real pixels. Phaser restores the full source box, so origin 0.5 is still the body center.
- **Look-dev**: `npm run art:sheet -- <out.png> <set> [scale]` renders a contact sheet (`torso`, `legs`, `weapons`, `gear`, `preview`, `hero`, `zombie:<type>`, `all-zombies`). `npm run art:chars` rebuilds only the characters. Generated previews: `art/preview/3d_*.png`.

Contract changes in the 3D set (the renderer handles both sets):
- Player torso and overlay frames are 224×224 (trimmed); legs 184×184. New torso pose `unarmed_walk_0..7`: arms counter-swing the legs; the renderer plays it in sync with `player_legs_walk_<i>` while unarmed and moving.
- Overlays are rendered **per torso frame**: `armor_head_<id>@<pose>_<i>`, `armor_body_<id>@<pose>_<i>`, `backpack_<itemId>@<pose>_<i>` (e.g. `backpack_small_backpack@long_idle_0`). The arms occlude them (depth-only pass), and they follow every pose exactly. The plain `armor_body_<id>` etc. still exist (unarmed idle) as a fallback. `torsoYaw` is 0 in `handsPerFrame`.
- `handsPerFrame[frame].hand` is the weapon grip point and `weaponAngle` the weapon's 2D angle in that frame; `weapon_<id>` origin is the grip. Muzzle = grip + weapon length in the idle pose (pistol muzzle ~34 world px ahead, matching the sim's bullet spawn at r+18).
- Zombie walk/attack/hit/pulse frames are 224 px (crawler 256, brute/bloater 288); death/corpse frames are 336 (crawler 304, brute/bloater 400) because a body lying at real proportions is ~1.7 m long. Names and frame counts are unchanged.

**FX** (atlas `fx`):
- `muzzle_handgun_0..2`, `muzzle_long_0..2`, `muzzle_shotgun_0..2`: bright core and flame petals, facing right.
- `blood_spray_0..5` (directional mist, facing right); `blood_drop_0..3` (particles); `blood_pool_0..5` (decals, irregular, dark, glossy edge); `blood_smear_0..2`.
- `spark_0..3`, `smoke_0..5` (soft puffs), `dust_0..3`, `shell_casing_0..1`, `shell_shotgun_0`.
- `explosion_0..11` (fireball, then smoke) and `scorch_0..1` (decal).
- `fire_0..7` (looping flame, used for burning zombies and the campfire), `ember_0..2`.
- `bullet_tracer` (a thin glowing streak, facing right) and `impact_wall_0..3` (chips/dust).
- `footprint_0` (subtle).

## B. Environment (ENVIRONMENT agent)
Every subtype that legacy world gen produces must have a sprite. Get the full list by reading `legacy/js/sv/sv-world.js` (`furn`, `cont`, `addCar`, `decor`, `furnishRoom`, `furnishSales`, district builders) and `legacy/js/sv/sv-worldrender.js`, which already draws each one procedurally. Use those as the reference for the sets and proportions. The new world.ts keeps the **same `sub` names**.

- **Tiles** (atlas `env_tiles`, 128×128 frames, seamless, at least 4 variants each):
  - Ground: asphalt, asphalt_cracked, sidewalk (concrete slabs), grass, grass_dry, dirt, gravel, forest_floor (leaves, needles), parking (concrete with oil).
  - Interior floors: wood, tile, carpet, concrete, linoleum, military.
  - Roofs: shingle_red, shingle_grey, shingle_brown, flat_gravel, flat_tar, metal.
- **Walls:** the tops of walls seen from above, as 9-slice-able strips: `wall_<material>_h` and `_v`, with caps for brick, concrete, siding, drywall, military and chainlink (see-through fence). `window_h`/`window_v` show glass with a frame. Doors are frames on the floor.
- **Road markings** are drawn by the renderer as vector paint, not sprites. Do provide a `decal_crosswalk` stripe texture and `decal_manhole`.
- **Props** (atlas `env_props`, one or more pages). Use top-down sprites at their real proportions, with fit hints:
  - Furniture. Living: couch/sofa, armchair, tv, table, bed, hbed, cot, nightstand, dresser, wardrobe, bookshelf, desk, sdesk, filing, plant. Kitchen and bathroom: stove, fridge, sink, counter, toilet, tub. Store: shelf, rack, cooler, booth, counter. Industrial: pallet, mcrate, crate, barrel, woodpile, ship (container). Outdoor: dumpster, pump (gas pump), planter, bench, sandbag, tower, fountain, statue, safe, locker, medcab, gunrack, guncase, toolbox, cabinet, clothes.
  - Cars (each in 4+ paint colors plus a burnt-out wreck): sedan, hatchback, pickup, van, police, ambulance, military truck, and a humvee for the military base.
  - Trees: oak/pine canopies in 3 sizes × 3 variants, layered leaf clumps with depth. Bushes, rocks and tufts.
  - Decals: blood, debris, leaves, cracks, oil, trash, bones, tire marks, puddle.
- **Structures** (atlas `structures`): bed, stash, workbench, wood_wall, metal_wall, wood_door (closed/open), barricade, spike_trap, campfire (stones and logs; the flame comes from fx `fire_*`), turret_base and turret_head (facing right).
- **Drops:** `drop_bag` (duffel/loot bag) and `drop_deathbag` (a red-tagged backpack).
- **Item icons:** see the output contract above. Draw them in the same top-down or 3/4 style on a transparent background.

## Quality bar
After generating, **look at your output**: open the PNGs with the Read tool, and write a contact-sheet PNG to `art/preview/` to inspect animation frames side by side.
- Iterate until characters read as people and zombies, and the walk cycles are clearly animated (no static frames).
- Iterate until materials look like materials (asphalt grain, wood planks, fabric folds, metal sheen), and the whole set shares one lighting direction and palette.
